# Prior art: kitsune, shrines, mechanical animals, and ringworlds

Tags: [src](URL) means the source was opened and read for this note. [est] means our own judgment.

## Kitsune folklore

- **Tails.** A fox gains tails with age and power: "a greater number of tails indicates an older and more powerful Kitsune." Folktales often say a new tail comes about every 100 years. One, five, seven, and nine tails are the most common counts. The nine-tailed fox and the thousand-year "heavenly fox" (*tenko*) come from Chinese sources [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitsune).
- **Good omens.** White foxes (*byakko*), black foxes, and nine-tailed foxes are all treated as good omens [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitsune).
- **Kitsunebi (fox-fire).** A kitsune can make fire by stroking or striking its tail, or in some regions by breathing it. Foxes use kitsunebi to lure travellers, like a will-o'-the-wisp [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitsune).
- **The fox jewel (*tama*).** A fox is said to carry a luminous jewel, often in its tail, that it needs for transformation and magic. Images of Inari foxes without it are rare. In one 18th-century anecdote a stolen jewel flashes kitsunebi until it is returned [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitsune).
- **Good and wild foxes.** Inari's white foxes were originally the god's messengers, "but the line between the two is now blurred." Folklore splits benevolent *zenko*, Inari's messengers, from wild *yako* or *nogitsune*, which are "often portrayed as mischievous or harmful" [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitsune).
- **Shapeshifting and possession.** After 50–100 years a fox can take human form, often by putting a leaf, reeds, or a skull on its head. It is exposed by a showing tail or by fear of dogs. Fox possession is called *kitsune-tsuki* [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitsune).

## Inari shrines and Fushimi Inari

- **The head shrine.** Fushimi Inari-taisha, founded by tradition in 711 by the Hata clan, is said to have as many as 32,000 sub-shrines across Japan [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fushimi_Inari-taisha).
- **Torii.** The shrine has about 10,000 torii, donated since the Edo period by people wishing for something or giving thanks. Around 800 form the Senbon Torii tunnel on the main path [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fushimi_Inari-taisha).
- **The mountain.** Trails wind 4 km up Mount Inari (233 m) past many smaller shrines, about a 2-hour walk. Tens of thousands of personal rock altars (*otsuka*) carry engraved names for Inari [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fushimi_Inari-taisha).
- **Fox statues.** Foxes are Inari's messengers, and statues often hold the rice-granary key in their mouths [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fushimi_Inari-taisha).

## Shinto architecture and placement

- **Layout.** A shrine is reached by the *sandō* approach road, through a torii that "marks the entrance to a sacred area." Visitors purify at the *chōzuya/temizuya* fountain, pray at the *haiden* worship hall, and the kami lives in the *honden*. The honden is closed to the public and holds the *shintai*, often a mirror. Paired *komainu* guardians (one mouth open, one closed), *tōrō* stone lanterns along the approach, and a *tamagaki* fence complete the grounds [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinto_architecture).
- **Placement.** The earliest sacred places were natural features: rocks, waterfalls, islands, and "especially mountains." Some early sites were marked only by a fence and a torii [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinto_architecture).
- **Building styles.** Shinmei (plain unfinished wood, Ise), Taisha (raised floor, central pillar, Izumo), Nagare (the most common, with a roof extended over the entrance), and Sumiyoshi (vermilion pillars, white walls). Ridge finials called *chigi* and *katsuogi* top the roofs [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinto_architecture).
- **Shimenawa.** These ropes, once hemp and mostly rice straw since 1948, purify and mark sacred space. They often carry zigzag *shide* paper, which represents lightning. They are tied around shrines, torii, sacred trees (homes of *kodama*), and spirit rocks (*iwakura*). Izumo Taisha's rope is 13.5 m long [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimenawa).

## Mechanical-animal design: Horizon Zero Dawn

- **Hunting, not war.** Art director Jan-Bart van Beek's GDC 2018 talk took the design from a few drawings through test models to the final creatures [src](https://gdcvault.com/play/1025049/Art-Direction-Bootcamp-A-No). Principles reported from it (via a third-party summary): the core feeling should be *hunting*, not war. Machines should read as life forms, not "soulless machines." They are based on real or extinct animals so players can predict behaviour, but behaviour matters more than literal likeness. Ecology shapes form, gameplay can overrule ecology, and the Thunderjaw took about 18 months [src](https://videohighlight.com/v/iDqDPsjrz94).
- **What Guerrilla said at launch** [src](https://blog.playstation.com/2017/02/24/the-making-of-horizon-zero-dawns-machines/):
  - Scorpion prototypes were dropped because thin legs gave "barely any surface to hit."
  - The Watcher should "resemble a combination of animals but not a literal translation of a singular one."
  - Large surfaces carry components that can be shot off. The Watcher's eye is a one-shot weak spot; the Scrapper has many weak points but no critical one.
  - Scanning reveals weak spots.
  - The 25 species form an ecology, and herds stay herds.
- **Inspirations and construction** [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machines_(Horizon)):
  - Thunderjaw from T. rex, Tallneck from a giraffe, Stalker from a maned wolf, Watcher from meerkats plus a security camera, Shellwalker from a hermit crab, Frostclaw from a red panda.
  - Terraforming machines are vertebrate-like. Military machines are insect, arachnid, or crustacean.
  - An outer frame with soft internals gives readable "soft spots."
  - Detached weapons can be picked up and used.
  - The audio team called its approach "natural-fiction," with the Watcher voiced as a "crazy Chihuahua."

## Ringworld touchstones

**Niven's Ringworld (1970).** It won the Nebula, Hugo, and Locus awards [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ringworld). Its dimensions:

- Radius about 1.5 × 10⁸ km (95 million mi).
- Circumference about 9.7 × 10⁸ km.
- Width about 1.6 million km.
- Rim walls 1,600 km (1,000 mi) high.
- Spin 1,200 km/s, giving about 0.99 g.
- Inner surface equal to about three million Earths.
[src](https://larryniven.fandom.com/wiki/Ringworld) [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ringworld)

Other details:

- **Scrith.** The floor and walls are made of scrith, a milky-grey, almost frictionless material that blocks 40% of neutrinos. The foundation is about 30 m deep [src](https://larryniven.fandom.com/wiki/Ringworld).
- **Night.** Night comes from an inner ring of shadow squares joined by thin, ultra-strong wire [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ringworld). We did not confirm a count of squares this session.
- **No horizon.** The surface curves upward, so at night the far ring shows as "a giant arch." Silt pumped from the seas piles into mountains at the base of the rim walls [src](https://www.h2g2.com/approved_entry/A87815604).
- **Great Oceans.** Two oceans hold 1:1 maps of other worlds [src](https://larryniven.fandom.com/wiki/Ringworld).
- **Fist-of-God.** A meteor strike pushed the floor up into a mountain whose summit, above the atmosphere, is "just a hole" [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ringworld).
- **Instability.** Fans chanted "The Ringworld is unstable!" at the 1971 Worldcon, which led to *The Ringworld Engineers* [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ringworld).

**Halo, Installation 04.** It is 10,000 km across with a 318 km wide band, 0.992 g, and controlled temperatures from −23 to 40 °C. Its biomes range from glaciers to seas, its weather is "highly unpredictable," and it is dark three-quarters of the time with "no sunrise or sunset" [src](https://www.halopedia.org/Installation_04). Large walls and chasms divide the surface, bridged by hard light, and a honeycomb interior lies beneath [src](https://www.halopedia.org/The_Anatomy_of_Halo).

**Halo's scale is the more usable one** [est]. A few-hundred-km band is something a player can believe they could cross. Niven's numbers are better as backdrop, in the sky and in the lore.

## What we take

1. **Tails as progression.** The player's fox companion (or the player's kitsune form) starts with one tail and gains a tail per shrine circuit, up to seven (one of folklore's common counts), with nine reserved for a mythic being. Each tail is a perk: stamina, warmth, glide distance, kitsunebi charge. [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitsune) [est]
2. **Kitsunebi as the light system.** Blue-white tail-fire is the player's lantern, ignition source (chemistry element), and wayfinding wisp. It is the only cool-hued fire in the game. A wild *yako* lures you off-path with the same light in a sicklier green, so trustworthy and untrustworthy light read differently. [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitsune) [est]
3. **The fox-jewel economy.** Collected *tama* power both kitsune magic and fox-bots. Fox-bot cores are literally fox jewels, which ties machines to myth. [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitsune) [est]
4. **A shrine kit from modular, instanced parts:**
   - torii (myōjin and shinmei variants), komainu or fox pair (key and jewel in mouth), tōrō lantern, chōzuya basin, haiden and honden boxes with chigi and katsuogi, tamagaki fence;
   - shimenawa built as a spline mesh with shide cards;
   - a "torii road" spline tool that instances N gates along a path and uses inscriptions for lore.
   Shrines sit on peaks, waterfalls, and spirit rocks bound with shimenawa, never randomly on flat ground. [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinto_architecture) [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimenawa) [est]
5. **The pilgrimage mountain.** One authored Inari-style mountain per region: a 2–4 km switchback through hundreds of torii past small shrines and otsuka, ending at a summit honden. This is the region's signature walk. [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fushimi_Inari-taisha)
6. **Fox-machine design rules, taken from Horizon:**
   - Base each bot on fox behaviour (pouncing on snow, curious head tilts, trotting in a pack) and blend it with one other animal per class: fox + crab for haulers, fox + heron for scouts, fox + wolf for hunters.
   - Hostile war-androids take insect and crustacean cues; benign or ancient fox-bots stay vertebrate.
   - Every bot has large hittable surfaces, two or three detachable parts, and one glowing weak point (the jewel).
   - The eye or jewel colour shows state: calm, curious, hostile. Shape language and sound give each bot a personality.
   [src](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machines_(Horizon)) [src](https://blog.playstation.com/2017/02/24/the-making-of-horizon-zero-dawns-machines/) [est]
7. **Ringworld scale: Halo band, Niven sky.**
   - The playable band is a few hundred km wide (start with one streamed segment about 8 km long). Rim walls are visible on both edges as pale, scrith-like cliffs that vanish into haze at altitude.
   - The ring arcs up into the sky by day and glows at night.
   - Shadow squares pass overhead and cause day/night, so sunsets are hard-edged eclipses.
   - Spill mountains rise at the wall bases. One Fist-of-God mountain open to space marks the far goal.
   - Weather can switch on "as if at the flip of a switch," as Halo's does, as a sign of machine control.
   [src](https://larryniven.fandom.com/wiki/Ringworld) [src](https://www.h2g2.com/approved_entry/A87815604) [src](https://www.halopedia.org/Installation_04) [est]
8. **Use Babylon.js for the sky arch.** Render the ring arch as a single, very large curved ribbon mesh in the far skybox layer, with its own fog-free shader. Never render it as real geometry at true scale. [est]
